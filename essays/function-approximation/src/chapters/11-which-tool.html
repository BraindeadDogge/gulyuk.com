Which tool, when

Several figures above have a "Pin to compare" button (chapters 1, 3, 4, 6, 7 and 8). Pin a few fits and they land here, drawn on the same target with their error, their parameter count and the time they took to compute.

The comparison that matters most is what happens outside the data (extrapolation). Here the same fits are drawn beyond the interval they were built on (shaded), where the target keeps going.

These are rules of thumb, not laws. Which row wins depends on how many inputs you have, how smooth the function is, how noisy the data is, and what a mistake costs. If you remember one thing from this page: every method here is a set of simple pieces (a dictionary) plus a rule for choosing the coefficients, and the interesting question is what each dictionary makes cheap and what it makes expensive.

# New idea checklist

Every idea sits in one **area** and carries up to three **topics**, all taken from the lists at the bottom of
this file. Reuse before you invent: the point is that the vocabulary stays small however much you write, so the
home graph stays readable and nobody (including you) has to remember which near-duplicate tag was used last time.

## Before you publish

1. **Pick one area.** Ask: where would a reader look for this? That is the area, even if the idea touches others.
   The area is also the hub the idea hangs from on the home graph, and the section it appears in on the plain list.
2. **Pick 0 to 3 topics from the list.** Prefer topics under the same area. A topic from another area is fine when
   it really applies (a career note about an AI tool can carry `llms`). Fewer is better; do not fill the slots.
3. **Nothing fits?** Read the whole list once more, other areas included. Still nothing: add a topic (next section).
   Do not add one for a single idea. Add it when you can name three more ideas you might write under it.
4. **Write the frontmatter** in `content/ideas/<slug>.md`. The filename is the URL and never changes.

   ```md
   ---
   title: What the idea is called
   summary: One or two sentences. Required.
   tagline: One short line, 80 characters at most. Required; it is printed on the idea's share picture.
   date: 2026-10-20
   area: ai                     # exactly one id from the list below
   topics: [ml-fundamentals]    # 0 to 3 ids from the list below
   kind: note                   # research | project | note | linkedin
   maturity: seedling           # seedling | budding | evergreen
   draft: true                  # remove when you are ready to publish
   ---
   ```

5. **Run `pnpm test`.** A wrong area or topic fails with the list of valid ones. The home page, the lists, the
   topic pages, RSS and the sitemap pick the idea up on the next build.

## Adding a topic

1. Add it under the right area in `content/taxonomy.yml`: an `id` (lowercase, hyphens, unique across all areas
   and topics), a `label` people would recognise, and a one-line `about`.
2. Run `pnpm taxonomy`. It rewrites the tables below. The tests fail if you forget, which is on purpose.
3. Use it in the idea you were writing.

## Adding an area (rare)

Areas are the hubs of the home graph; around six is the right number. A new area needs a reason: it has to hold
ideas that none of the existing areas can. If a topic would do, add a topic. If you do add an area, do the same
steps as for a topic, and check where the CV entries in `content/cv.yml` should link to it.

## Renaming or retiring a topic

Change the `label` freely. Changing an `id` changes the address of its page, so only do it when you must: the build
then fails and lists every idea that still uses the old one. To retire a topic, move its ideas to another one first.
